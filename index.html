What's left out, and why

  • Only pairs that follow the simple rules are included: metals lose all their valence electrons, nonmetals gain or share until they have 8 (hydrogen: 2), and every compound shown really exists with that formula.
  • Transition metals, metalloids, noble gases, and the radioactive Fr and Ra are greyed out.
  • Beryllium is a metal, but its compounds (like BeCl₂) are covalent, so it's left out.
  • Aluminum pairs only with oxygen and fluorine. Its chloride, bromide, iodide, sulfide, and nitride are largely covalent.
  • Nitrides appear only for lithium and the group 2 metals; the other alkali metals don't form stable ones. Metals with H, C, or P (hydrides, carbides, phosphides) are left out.
  • Nonmetal pairs are left out when the octet rule doesn't predict the real molecule: N + O and S + O (NO, NO₂, SO₂…), P + O, C + N, sulfur fluorides (SF₆), and unstable ones like NBr₃ and NI₃. C, P, S, and Se bonded to themselves form chains and rings, not simple molecules.
